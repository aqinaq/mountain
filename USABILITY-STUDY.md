# Mountain usability study — five-reader protocol

This document is ready to use, but it is intentionally not pre-filled with
fictional findings. Recruit five people who read English as an additional
language; aim for a mix of phone/desktop use and self-rated proficiency.

## Research question

Can a first-time reader import something, understand and save an unfamiliar
word, then find and complete its review without coaching?

## Session format

- 20 minutes, moderated remotely or in person.
- Use a fresh anonymous account and the participant’s preferred device.
- Ask for permission to take notes and record the screen. Do not record faces or
  collect names; identify sessions as P1–P5.
- Use the same EPUB and public article URL for all sessions. Include one
  unsupported `.docx` so the recovery path is observed consistently.
- Ask the participant to think aloud. Do not explain controls unless they are
  blocked for 60 seconds; record any intervention.

## Tasks and success criteria

| Task | Prompt | Success without help | Capture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Import | “Add this book to your library.” | Opens the supplied EPUB | Completion, time, wrong turns |
| Understand | “Find out what an unfamiliar word means here.” | Taps a word and reads the explanation | Completion, time, expectation mismatches |
| Save | “Keep that word so you can learn it later.” | Saves from the explanation card | Completion, confidence (1–5) |
| Review | “Practice the word you just saved.” | Finds Vocabulary and answers a card | Completion, time, navigation path |
| Recover | “Try adding this file, then decide what to do.” | Understands the unsupported-file error | Can explain cause and next action |
| Privacy | “What is stored, and what happens if you remove the book?” | Finds the disclosure and answers accurately | Correct/partial/incorrect |

Afterward ask: “What felt uncertain?”, “What did you expect to happen but did
not?”, and “Would you trust this with a book you own? Why or why not?”

## Reporting template

Report the denominator with every result: for example, “4/5 found Vocabulary
without help.” Separate observed facts from interpretation. For each finding,
include severity, supporting sessions, the design change, and the follow-up
check. Do not turn preference comments into claims about learning outcomes.
